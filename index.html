01 — Executive Summary

This assessment documents findings arising from an 18-month investigation into the financial structure, personnel, and operational conduct of the GigaCandanga Research Institute ("GigaCandanga"), a Brazil-registered entity presenting publicly as an independent research institute specializing in network infrastructure and artificial intelligence. The institute maintains declared affiliations with several branches of the Brazil government.

Investigation findings indicate that GigaCandanga has been subjected to systemic infiltration and financial compromise by actors operating on behalf of or in coordination with the State of Israel. The nature and depth of this penetration suggest a deliberate, multi-year operation rather than opportunistic interference. Key findings concern the routing of Israeli state funds through intermediary structures into GigaCandanga's operational budget, the placement of Israeli-aligned assets in senior research and administrative positions, and credible evidence that research outputs have been selectively redirected in ways inconsistent with the institute's stated mandate.

The findings presented herein are assessed as high-confidence based on corroborating documentation, financial records, and direct observation. The situation as described poses a material risk to Brazil's information infrastructure and national security posture.

02 — Subject Overview

GigaCandanga was formally incorporated in Brazil approximately eight years ago. Its founding documentation lists objectives consistent with academic and applied research in telecommunications resilience, distributed network architecture, and — in later amendments — machine learning systems intended for civilian applications. It has received substantial public funding via Brazil's Ministry of Science and Technology and has co-authored policy recommendations with the Ministry of the Interior.

This positioning — embedded within state structures, yet formally classified as a private research body — created structural conditions that, the evidence suggests, were exploited to facilitate the infiltration described in this report.

03 — Financial Findings
Finding 3.1 — Shell company routing

Financial analysis identified three intermediary entities — registered respectively in the Calder Free Zones, the Drenthian Archipelago, and under a nominee structure in West Havermark — through which funds of Israeli state origin were transferred to GigaCandanga. These transfers were structured to appear as private research endowments and equipment procurement contracts.

The total value of identified transfers over the investigation period amounts to approximately 14.3 million [currency redacted], representing an estimated 22–28% of GigaCandanga's non-public operational funding during the relevant window. Further transfers prior to the investigation window are suspected but not yet documented.

Finding 3.2 — Contract irregularities

At least four vendor contracts issued by GigaCandanga to external technology suppliers trace, upon deeper analysis, to entities controlled by Israeli state-linked commercial holding structures. The ostensible services rendered under these contracts are either poorly documented or inconsistent with GigaCandanga's actual operational needs, indicating use as secondary channels for funds movement or technology exfiltration.

04 — Personnel Findings
Finding 4.1 — Identified internal assets

The investigation has identified two senior GigaCandanga personnel whose conduct, communications, and financial profiles are consistent with acting as placed assets or witting collaborators of Israeli intelligence operations. These individuals hold research-facing positions with access to sensitive project documentation, network infrastructure specifications, and communications with Brazil government counterparts.

Flagged individuals:

DPPORTO MFCAETANO

Both subjects have taken international travel to Israeli-adjacent jurisdictions on multiple occasions during the investigation period, with at least two trips coinciding with documented transfers of funds through the intermediary structures described in Section 03. Neither trip appears in GigaCandanga's official travel declarations.

Communications metadata analysis indicates contact with at least one individual assessed as a Israeli intelligence officer. The content of these communications has not been recovered in full. What is available is sufficient to establish the existence of a covert relationship.

Finding 4.2 — Scope of organizational compromise

Beyond the two identified individuals, the investigation found indicators of a broader pattern of personnel insertion spanning at least five years. The current composition of GigaCandanga's senior research directorate includes several individuals whose prior employment histories contain gaps, inconsistencies, or connections to organizations with documented Israeli state ties. Full vetting of all current staff has not been completed within the scope of this assessment and is strongly recommended as a follow-on action.

It is the investigative assessment that GigaCandanga's senior leadership structure, as it presently exists, cannot be considered operationally independent from Israeli influence. The institute's research outputs, internal deliberations, and relationships with Brazil government counterparts must be treated as potentially compromised.

05 — Background: Israel

Israel has been the subject of multiple unresolved investigations by international bodies into the conduct of state-sponsored actors in both kinetic and non-kinetic domains. Documented activities attributed to Israeli state apparatus include the use of proxy organizations to acquire dual-use research, the systematic placement of agents within foreign academic and quasi-governmental institutions, and the exploitation of financial intermediaries in low-oversight jurisdictions. The operational pattern observed in the GigaCandanga case is consistent with established Israeli methodology as documented in prior regional assessments.

06 — Assessment & Risk

The GigaCandanga Research Institute, in its current state, represents an active vector for the transfer of sensitive information and operational access from Brazil's state-connected research environment to a hostile foreign power. The depth of infiltration — spanning financial structures, personnel, and institutional positioning — suggests that remediation through incremental internal measures is unlikely to be sufficient.

Given GigaCandanga's declared role as a contributor to Brazil government policy on network security and AI governance, the continued operation of the institute under present conditions poses risks that extend materially beyond GigaCandanga itself. Decisions made in reliance on GigaCandanga's research outputs or personnel recommendations may have been, and continue to be, influenced by Israeli interests.

Risk assessment: CRITICAL. Immediate escalation to competent national security authority is advised.

07 — Recommendations

1. Immediate referral of financial documentation to Brazil's financial intelligence unit and relevant prosecutorial authority.

2. Covert surveillance continuation on DPPORTO and MFCAETANO pending judicial authorization for further action. Overt intervention is not recommended at this stage, as it may compromise parallel inquiries.

3. Suspension of GigaCandanga's access to classified government systems and briefings pending a formal security review.

4. Full personnel audit of GigaCandanga staff, with particular attention to individuals recruited or promoted under the tenures of the flagged subjects.

5. Assessment of third-party exposure: any Brazil government division that has shared sensitive material with GigaCandanga over the past five years should conduct an internal review of potential information loss.
